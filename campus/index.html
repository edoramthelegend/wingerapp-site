Wybber Campus Ambassador

Last updated: 2026-10-19

Start Wybber on your campus. A campus ambassador is a student who brings Wybber to their own campus: tells friends and clubs about it, helps with setup and hands out invite codes.

Where things stand: Wybber isn’t on the App Store or Google Play yet. We’re taking ambassador applications now.

What you get

  • A founding-ambassador title and a reference letter. The first ambassadors are known as Wybber’s founding ambassadors.
  • Lifetime Infinity. Your own account and the first 5 people who directly use your ambassador code get the Infinity plan for life.
  • A monthly campus leaderboard. There’s a monthly ranking on your campus and across all campuses. You appear under an alias such as “Elçi #1”; your name is shown only if you agree.
  • A revenue share. 10% of gross revenue for 12 months, counted from the first payment of each device you invite. Gross revenue: what the user paid, excluding VAT, before the app store’s fee. Details are in the ambassador contract.

Good to know

  • There are at most 10 ambassadors per campus.
  • Only ambassadors who sign a contract with Wybber earn the revenue share. Ordinary users earn nothing from an invite; passing a code to a friend pays no one.
  • Every user shares invites through 5 codes that each work once; an ambassador code can be used by many people.
  • In the ambassador panel you see the total number of people who joined through you, never who is who. Counts below 10 aren’t shown, and larger ones are rounded down to a multiple of 5.
  • Wybber is for people aged 18 and over, and you must be 18 or older to be an ambassador.

How to apply

Write your application email

In the email that opens, tell us your name, your university and campus, your department and year, that you’re 18 or older, and how you’d bring Wybber to your campus, in a few sentences. Don’t send an ID number, an address or bank details. We read each application and get back to you.

More about ambassadors’ data is in section 13 of the Privacy Policy.

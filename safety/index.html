Wybber Safety and Moderation Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-19

This policy describes what Wybber actually does today to help keep people safe; it doesn't promise features that don't exist yet. The Turkish version is the primary text; this English version has the same meaning. The rules everyone agrees to are in the Terms of Service and, in plain words, in the Community Guidelines.

1. Age and identity

  • Wybber is only for people 18 or older. When you sign up, you confirm that you are over 18.
  • Your age comes from the age range you choose at sign-up (for example 18-22). We don't check an official ID document — a limit that is common in the industry, and we state it honestly.
  • Your account is a cryptographic key created on your phone; we don't ask for an e-mail address or phone number. You can bring the same account to a new phone with your 12 recovery words, or move it from your old phone with a QR code.

2. The "Liveness verified" badge

  • You can choose to do a short liveness check with your camera: you're asked to blink, smile and turn your head left and right. More than one of these steps has to pass for the check to be accepted; the screen doesn't say in detail which step failed.
  • It runs entirely on your phone (Apple Vision on iPhone, Google ML Kit on Android). The photo, the video frames and your face data are never sent to our server.
  • Only iPhone compares you with your profile photo, using a face-recognition model that runs on the phone. Android has no calibrated comparison model, so on Android the badge makes no claim about matching your profile photo.
  • The result is saved on your phone as a record signed with your own account key (when, how, the liveness result, the match score and a comparison summary). The photo, video and face data are never part of it; the record is not sent to our server and is stored encrypted on your phone. What reaches your match's phone is not the record but a short signed statement that you passed the liveness check (no score, no photo comparison).
  • What the badge says: "Liveness verified" — never "identity verified". It shows that a live person was in front of the camera; it isn't an identity check. A match with the profile photo is claimed only when the iPhone comparison ran and your main profile photo matched; otherwise the badge says "Liveness verified — profile photo not matched".
  • Honest limit: the badge makes it harder to use someone else's or an old photo, but it is not a guarantee. The check against photos or videos held up to the camera hasn't yet been calibrated on large real-world samples, so it doesn't fully protect against, for example, a video played from another screen or a carefully prepared print.

3. Reporting and blocking

Where to find it. In any chat, open the ⋯ menu and choose Report or Block; or tap Report or block in a match's details. These options are there once a match has been opened for chat; there is no separate report screen during a Wybber conversation. Settings → Privacy and safety → Blocked users shows the people you've blocked and lets you unblock them, and Settings → Privacy and safety → Safety center explains all of this in the app.

What happens when you report someone:

  1. You pick a reason: harassment, spam, fake profile, inappropriate content, scam, violence or other. Reports about violence, a scam or harassment are sent with one tap and block the person at the same moment; the other reasons ask you to confirm first and don't block automatically.
  2. Your phone sends the reported person's identity only as a one-way hash. Who made the report is always worked out on our server from your own sign-in — whatever the app says about the reporter is ignored — so nobody can file a report in someone else's name.
  3. Our server keeps the report: the two hashed identities, the reason, its status ("pending") and the time. No written description is collected, and the app doesn't attach screenshots or message text, so no content or evidence reaches our server.
  4. If you're offline or our server doesn't answer, the report waits on your phone and is sent the next time the app is opened or comes back to the foreground. Until it reaches us, it isn't in our records (or in your data copy).

Seeing your own reports. There is no separate report-history screen. The reports you filed are in your data copy (Settings → Privacy and safety → Download your data; a new copy can be prepared once an hour): for each one, the reported person's hashed identity, the reason, the evidence field, the status and the time. It shows no names and not the outcome: the status is "pending" until an operator has reviewed the report and "resolved" after that; the decision itself and who reviewed it aren't included, because they are data about other people. A reviewed report is deleted 90 days after review (no action or a warning) or 365 days after review (suspension), and then no longer appears; a report that hasn't been reviewed yet isn't deleted. Reports filed about you aren't included — a deliberate limit that protects the person who reported (Privacy Policy §6).

Who reviews, and how fast: Every report is reviewed by a person — a Wybber operator — within 24 hours of reaching our server. Reports waiting for review are monitored. The operator sees only what our server holds: the reason, the time, the two hashed identities and how many reports the same account has received. Your messages aren't on our server, so the operator can't read them, and nothing on our server scans content automatically. The operator closes each report with one of three decisions: no action; a warning — if notifications are on for their phone, the reported person gets one neutral notification ("We received a report about your account. Please follow the community guidelines."), which names neither you, the reason nor any content; or suspension — the reported account is closed, can no longer sign in or connect, and the other pending reports about it are closed with the same decision. You aren't told the decision (see "Seeing your own reports" for what your data copy shows). If your account was suspended and you think it's a mistake, write to support@wybber.com; an operator can lift a suspension. The 24 hours start when the report reaches our server — for a report that waited on your phone while you were offline (step 4), when it is sent. Reporting is not an emergency service: in an emergency, call your local emergency number (§6), and don't rely on reports for urgent help. To contact us about anything else: support@wybber.com.

Blocking:

  • Anyone can block anyone, one-sidedly. The block is saved on your phone first and then sent to our server; if our server can't be reached, it waits and is retried. The matching and connection effects below start once our server has it.
  • Blocking is silent: neither the app nor our server tells the blocked person. You can see and undo your blocks; the blocked person simply can't see you, match with you or message you again.
  • When you block someone, any match between you is deleted at the same moment, so for the other person "I was blocked" and "the match ended" look the same — on purpose. Unblocking doesn't bring the match back.
  • Matching and connection setup always check blocks in both directions: a blocked person isn't suggested in a new Wybber round and can't connect to your phone.
  • When an account is deleted, the reports it filed or received are not deleted by the deletion (they follow the schedule in "Seeing your own reports"), but in them the deleted account's ID is replaced with a pseudonym that only our server key can link back to it (the other person's ID doesn't change unless they delete their own account) — so nobody can hide their report history by deleting their account. Because this pseudonym doesn't match your account ID, those reports also don't appear in a later data copy of yours.

4. Message protection on your phone (Guardian)

  • Wybber has a rule engine called Guardian that runs on your phone. It checks what Wybber writes for you (Wybber conversations, reply suggestions) and the chat messages with the person you matched with: a message you send before it leaves your phone, and a message you receive before it is shown to you.
  • Contact details are yours to share. Sharing a phone number, e-mail address or social media account in a chat is your decision; they are delivered as you wrote them. Before your first message with such details to a person, and only once, you see a short safety note ("Before you share your number: getting to know them a little and meeting somewhere busy the first time is a good idea."). "Send" sends the message at once and "Not now" leaves it in the text box; whichever you tap, the note isn't shown again for that person. In text that Wybber writes itself, phone numbers and e-mail addresses are still hidden.
  • Financial and account details are hidden. A card number, IBAN, card security code (CVV), verification or one-time code, password, Turkish national ID number (TC kimlik numarası: an 11-digit number with valid check digits) and Spanish DNI/NIE number (with a valid check letter) are replaced with a placeholder before they reach the other side: [CARD], [IBAN], [CODE] or [ID] — these are what scammers ask for most, and no match needs them. On your own screen your message appears as you typed it, with a note under it saying that these details were hidden from the other person.
  • Honest limits of hiding. Hiding recognizes patterns, not meaning, so some details can get through unhidden. A card number, IBAN or Turkish national ID number is hidden only when its check digits are valid; a Turkish national ID number has to be written without spaces, and a Spanish DNI/NIE number is hidden only when its check letter is right. A card number or Turkish national ID number starting with 0, and any number starting with +, is treated as a phone number and is not hidden. A Spanish DNI can start with 0 (for example 01234567L) and is hidden when its check letter is right; it has to be written without spaces inside the number (dots, a hyphen, or one space before a capital check letter are fine). Codes and passwords are caught when they follow a keyword (for example "verification code 482913" or "password: ..."; a security code written right after a card number is hidden too); codes written with spaces, such as "55 21 90", are not caught. The best protection is not to share these details with anyone.
  • Notes on messages you receive. The first time a person sends you contact details or a link to another app (for example a t.me, wa.me or Instagram link), you see a note, once for that person, reminding you that Wybber's protection (hiding card, IBAN and code details, blocking) doesn't work in another app. When a message — yours or theirs — asks for or mentions an ID number, card details, a code or a password, a gentle reminder is shown once for that person. When a message you receive seems to ask you for money, sounds like a possible threat, or talks about easy money or an investment, you see a one-time tip for that person and topic (for example: "This person seems to be asking you for money. Don't send money to someone you haven't met; Wybber will never ask you for money in a chat."); the sender isn't told. These notes never change or stop the message.
  • Messages that match its rules for spam, abuse or hate speech aren't sent: next to the English rules, Turkish and Spanish word lists catch profanity, insults, hate speech and threats, and the Spanish list also stops investment-scam invitations ("invierte conmigo", "duplica tu dinero"). Requests for money that the English, Spanish and Turkish rules recognize (for example "lend me some money", "I need $300 for rent", "mándame 50 euros" or "bana 500 lira gönderir misin"), phrases that can be a threat but are also ordinary sentences ("I know where you live", "sé dónde vives", "nerede çalıştığını biliyorum"), threats to share someone's photos or to hurt them that the lists recognize ("I'll post your photos", "fotoğraflarını yayınlarım", "te voy a hacer daño") and talk of easy money aren't stopped: the message is delivered and the person who receives it sees the one-time tip described above. Suggestions to meet bring a one-time tip about meeting safely. It screens for manipulation attempts only in text that Wybber itself generates (negotiation output, reply suggestions, icebreakers); this check doesn't run on person-to-person messages.
  • Honest limit: Guardian works with keyword and pattern rules — mainly in English, plus Turkish and Spanish word lists. It doesn't understand meaning and it misses things; it is not full content moderation. Reporting and blocking (§3) are your main protection. If you think one of your messages was held back by mistake, write to support@wybber.com.
  • Guardian checks text on your own phone; its decisions and logs stay on your phone. In our review of the app we found nothing that sends these decisions to our server (this is a review of the code, not a formal audit).
  • Wybber conversations between two Wybbers use fixed, predefined answer codes, not free text, and Wybber Intelligence is not part of the decision.

5. We can't read your chats — what that means for requests from authorities

  • All messages between matched users are end-to-end encrypted and go directly between the two phones. If a direct connection isn't possible, the traffic passes through Wybber's own relay server, which can't read it but sees IP addresses while relaying. The technical details exchanged to set up the connection also contain IP addresses; they pass through our server without being stored (Privacy Policy §3.2).
  • Our server keeps only who talked with whom, and when (internal account numbers) and whether a conversation is active — there is nowhere on our server for message content.
  • So Wybber cannot hand over the content of anyone's chats in response to any request, including a court order: the content never reached us, and we hold no key to decrypt it. What we can lawfully provide is the information we actually hold, for example: sign-up details (account ID, the gender, gender of interest and age range given at sign-up, the province and district chosen), who matched or talked with whom and when, notification tokens, purchase records (product, the store's transaction number, a receipt hash, status) and report records (with hashed identities and no content). This list is an example; the full, current list is in Privacy Policy §3. We explain this limit in the same way to users and to authorities.

6. Meeting safely

These tips are also in the app (Settings → Privacy and safety → Safety center). The app doesn't show them automatically when a chat opens; the first time you share contact details in a chat, it shows a short note (§4).

  • Meet somewhere busy and public the first time.
  • Tell someone you trust where you're going and who you're meeting.
  • Arrange your own way there and back.
  • Take your time before sharing your number or address; never send money or financial details.
  • If someone asks for money, pushes for personal details early on or their story doesn't add up, use Report or Block (§3).
  • Trust your instincts; you can leave at any time.
  • In an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 in Türkiye). Wybber is not an emergency service, and reports are not monitored in real time.

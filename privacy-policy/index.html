Wybber Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-20

This policy explains, in plain language, what Wybber does with your information: what isn't kept on our servers, what reaches our server and why, who else receives anything, how long we keep it, and how you can see, download or delete it. The Turkish version is the primary text; this English version has the same meaning. Every statement here has been checked against how the app and our server actually work; a separate technical appendix that links each statement to the relevant part of our system is kept for auditors and regulators.

Who we are. The data controller is Dora AI Labs LLC, the operator of the Wybber app: a limited liability company organized under the laws of the State of Wyoming, United States, Wyoming Entity ID 2026-002106086. Contact: legal@wybber.com; for support and requests about your data, support@wybber.com. A postal address for correspondence will be added to this policy and to the KVKK Disclosure Notice; until then, requests are accepted by e-mail only.

At a glance

With you, not with us. Your profile (name, bio, photos), your answers to the preference questions, your messages, and your face-verification video and photo aren't kept on our servers. Wybber works out the matching itself — who suits you — on your phone, not on our servers. Your messages, your profile card once you match, and the photo and answers you choose to share travel end-to-end encrypted directly from your phone to your match's phone, so we cannot read that traffic. Your face-verification video and photo are never uploaded anywhere.

What goes to our server, and why. The items below, each with its reason; the sign-up form also sends a district and a country, which are stored but never used for matching. The full list is in §1 and §3:

  • an account ID that your phone creates at random (it is not your name, number or e-mail), mostly in a scrambled, one-way form (a "hash"); your account is opened right at the start of the sign-up screens, straight after the KVKK and consent step (§1.4);
  • the gender, gender of interest, what you're looking for (only dating, friends or both — never the kind of relationship), who you'd like to meet as friends if you chose friendship, age range and location you set (a province and, if you choose, a district; never a GPS coordinate) — to find match candidates; these go to the server as you answer the sign-up screens, together with the name of the step you are on (not your answer) — to help you finish an unfinished sign-up (§1.4);
  • if you used an invite code during the launch phase: a pseudonymous record of the code and of who invited you (§1.5); if you are a campus ambassador, or one of the first 5 people to use an ambassador's code, the record of your lifetime Infinity entitlement (§1.6);
  • your plan and purchases — to give you what you paid for and to keep the accounting records the law requires;
  • a scrambled ID for your phone — to stop one phone from claiming several free trials;
  • your notification token — to send you notifications; if you switch on "Campaigns and reminders" yourself, that choice, the times you switched it on and off, the version id of the text shown next to the switch and how many come-back reminders have been sent to you as well (§1.7);
  • whether a match happened (never why), who you blocked or reported (as scrambled IDs), and the technical details needed to connect two phones;
  • the dates (no time of day, kept 45 days) of the days you open the app in the foreground — to count, in total, how many people use Wybber (§3.4);
  • feedback you choose to send us, and — as with any online service — technical request logs, which include your IP address.

Your photos, your messages and your answers to the preference questions aren't kept on our servers.

Who else receives data.

  • Apple or Google — to check your purchases and to deliver notifications. On Android, Google's on-device face-detection library may also send Google diagnostic data (never images).
  • Hugging Face — only if you download Wybber Intelligence's advanced language features, which are not available in version 1.0 (§2.2); it sees your IP address while the file downloads.
  • Our own relay server — when two phones can't connect directly, the encrypted traffic passes through it; it sees IP addresses, never content.
  • Your match — only what you choose to share, sent encrypted straight to their phone.

We don't sell your data or share it with advertisers, and the app contains no advertising or analytics trackers.

How long. Most of your account data is deleted as soon as you delete your account. If you leave sign-up unfinished and take no step for 90 days, your account is deleted automatically (§1.4). A few records are kept longer, because the law requires it or to prevent abuse; §5 and §6 list each one and why.

Your choices and rights.

  • See or download your data: Settings → Privacy and safety → Download your data.
  • Delete your account: Settings → Delete account.
  • Withdraw a consent: Settings → Privacy and safety (gender and interest-gender consent, sensitive preference consent) or Settings → Matching → Let my Wybber talk for me. The consent you give for come-back reminders is withdrawn by switching off Settings → Notifications → Notification settings → Campaigns and reminders; logging out also switches the setting off and records it as a withdrawal (§1.7).
  • Anything else — a correction, an objection, a question: support@wybber.com. You can also complain to a data-protection authority (§12).

1. Our principle, and the exceptions

Wybber is built so that your personal information is processed on your phone, not on our servers. When something has to go to our server, we send identifiers in a scrambled, one-way form (a SHA-256 hash) wherever we can. There are deliberate exceptions, and we list them openly:

  1. Your matching details, as they are. As you answer each sign-up screen, your gender, gender of interest, city and age range go to our server unscrambled (§1.4), because the server needs them to find candidates. What you're looking for goes too, but only in its coarse form: dating, friends or both (someone looking only for dating and someone looking only for friends are never paired); the kind of relationship you want (serious, casual…) is not sent to our server. If you chose friendship, who you'd like to meet as friends goes too. Gender of interest and the gender you chose for friendship can indicate your sexual orientation, so they rest on your explicit consent (§9 and the Explicit Consent Notice §1). You can change what you're looking for at most once a day; for that the server keeps only the time of your last change. The sign-up form also sends a district and a country; these are stored, but matching never uses them and they are not shown back to the app. We plan to remove them.
  2. Your location is a province, never a coordinate. The server stores your province (and, if you choose, your district) as a number from a public list. It does not accept GPS coordinates: a request that contains one is rejected, and there is nowhere to store one. (An early version stored coordinates; that storage was removed in September 2026 and the old values were destroyed with it.) See §1.2.
  3. Purchases. When you buy something, the store receipt goes to Apple or Google to be checked; that is how in-app purchases work.
  4. A scrambled ID for your phone is recorded when your account is opened, which is at the start of the sign-up screens — see §1.1.
  5. Travel Mode. On the Link and Infinity plans, the city you choose for Travel Mode and its end time go to the server — see §1.3.
  6. Sign-up step and invite records. The name of the sign-up step you are on (not your answer) goes to the server; if you use an invite code during the launch phase, a pseudonymous record of the code and of who invited you goes too — see §1.4, §1.5, §1.6.

Your profile, preference answers, chats, face verification and the match calculations, on the other hand, aren't kept on our servers: they are with you, not with us. When you match, your name, age range and city — and, once you agree, your bio and photo — travel end-to-end encrypted directly to your match's phone. The six items above are not everything our server has: §3 lists everything it keeps, and why (for example your plan, your notification token and report records). The content of your messages never reaches us. Two small facts about chats do pass through our server, and we explain each one: the reminder about a chat waiting for a reply (§3.1), and the marker that lets a message reach a match who was offline (§3.3). Our server also counts events it sees itself (for example, how many matches happened) as totals that are not linked to anyone, and keeps three yes/no markers and the dates of the days you use the app on your account (§3.4). Like any online service, our server keeps technical logs of requests, which include IP addresses (§3). If you download Wybber Intelligence's advanced language features (not available in version 1.0), Hugging Face sees your IP address while it downloads (§2.2, §4).

1.1 Your phone's ID, and several accounts on one phone

When you sign up, our server records a scrambled, one-way ID for your phone — not your name, phone number or advertising ID — together with the day the phone was first seen (no time of day). This makes it harder to claim several free trials, or to reuse an invite code, on the same phone. This ID used to be sent only when you entered an invite code or started a trial; it is now sent at sign-up.

The ID usually comes from your phone's operating system. In rare cases — for example right after an iPhone restarts and before it is first unlocked, or on some modified Android systems — the app uses a random value it creates instead. For each phone we also note which of the two it was; for phones set up before we started noting this, we don't know.

Because the same ID is recorded for every account opened on a phone, we can technically tell that two accounts came from the same phone. We only use this to limit trial and invite-code abuse and to count a campus ambassador's share period per phone (§1.5). We do not use it to merge profiles, matches or chats, and blocking one account is not linked to any other account. Couples, flatmates, family members and buyers of second-hand phones share devices, and treating "same phone" as "same person" would be wrong and unfair.

Separately: a new account cannot see an older account's matches or chats on the same phone, and chat content was never on our server in the first place (§2).

This device record is not deleted when you delete your account. It is kept for 90 days after the last account was registered on that phone (not after your last use), so that deleting and reinstalling the app doesn't give a fresh free trial.

Backups. Our database is backed up daily. Backups are kept on our server for 7 days and, if an off-site copy is in place, off-site for up to 30 days. Deleting your account takes effect in the live database immediately, but a backup made before that can contain a copy until it is replaced in this normal cycle.

1.2 Your location: province and district (GPS optional)

In Settings → Matching → My location you set your province (required) and, if you like, your district. Candidates are always searched where you are: your own province — or, while Travel Mode is on, your Travel Mode city (§1.3). On the Search location screen the only choice is to put a district within your own province first (optional): people in that district are ordered first, then the rest of the province; nobody is left out just because of their district. If you leave it blank, your whole province is searched in the same order. Searching another province is possible only with Travel Mode; otherwise the server refuses it. Province and district are sent as numbers from a fixed public list, not as free text.

An older search choice. If before 13 October 2026 you picked a different province (or a district in it) on the Search location screen, that value stays on your account but is no longer used in matching; your next choice on that screen replaces it. It appears in your data copy and is deleted with your account (§6).

Changing your province. Setting your province the first time is free; after that you can change it at most twice in any 30 days, so that people can't hop between cities without Travel Mode. To apply this limit the server keeps only the times of your changes — not your previous provinces. A time older than 30 days is not used; each one is deleted by an hourly clean-up once it is 30 days old. Your data copy includes the change times we still hold, and all of them are deleted with your account (§6). Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping the service rules from being bypassed (KVKK Art. 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).

GPS is a shortcut, never a requirement. If you tap "Use my location", the app asks for location permission. If you allow it, your phone reads its position and turns it into a province/district name by itself, using a list built into the app, without going online. The coordinate is never sent to us and never stored; the name is only suggested on screen, and nothing is saved until you confirm. We deliberately don't use Apple's or Google's own location lookup, which would send your coordinate to them. If permission is refused, location services are off, it takes too long or no confident match is found, you simply choose by hand.

Sharing your district is a separate choice (off by default). Your province is enough for matching. If you turn district sharing on but your district has few residents, or too few people of your age range and gender are active there right now, the app automatically falls back to province level and tells you — so that you can't be singled out in a tiny group. Which districts qualify is recalculated every time from the number of people currently active; nothing is worked out in advance or stored.

Matching uses your province as a filter alongside gender and age range, when both of you have set a location and your search settings suit each other. A district is not a filter; it is only an ordering preference (people in the district you chose first, then the rest of the province) and is never shown to others. The old free-text city and district fields of the sign-up form (item 1 above) are separate and are still not used by matching.

Province and district numbers are sent unscrambled on purpose: there are only 81 provinces and about 970 districts in Türkiye, all public, so a hash would give a false sense of protection. The real protection is the small-group check above.

1.3 Travel Mode — searching in another city (Link and Infinity plans)

Travel Mode is included in the Link and Infinity plans and in the free trial; the Free and Spark plans don't include it. While Travel Mode is on, for matching purposes you are in the city you chose: people there can be matched with you, and you with them. Meanwhile you are not in your own province's pool, and district ordering is not used (Travel Mode works at city level).

What data: only two values — the number of the city you chose (from a fixed public list; any country) and the Travel Mode end time (the end of a period of 1 to 14 days that you pick). They are kept as one entry on your account; a new Travel Mode period replaces the previous one. There are no coordinates and no Travel Mode history, and GPS or your phone's location is not used for this.

Why, and legal basis: Travel Mode is a feature of your plan (or trial) that you ask for, and we need these two values to find candidates in the city you chose. Legal basis: entering into and performing our contract with you (KVKK Art. 5/2-c; GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).

Who sees it: our server doesn't tell anyone your Travel Mode city or that you are using Travel Mode, and the candidate lists sent to others contain no location. Matching uses it only to choose candidates — but because Travel Mode places you in that city's pool, people there can be matched with you.

How long: Travel Mode ends at the end time you chose. If your plan stops including Travel Mode (your subscription or trial ends, a refund, the end of a campaign), Travel Mode stops being used in matching from that moment and you return to your own province; the entry stays on your account until the end time, and if your plan comes back before then (renewal, restore, a new campaign) Travel Mode applies again for the time that is left. Once the end time has passed, an hourly clean-up deletes the entry. If you end Travel Mode yourself (even after your plan has lapsed), both values are deleted at once.

Data copy and deletion: while the entry exists — even after your plan has lapsed — your data copy shows your Travel Mode city (number and name) and end time. It is deleted with your account (§6).

Who this policy covers. §1–§12 cover people who use Wybber as a dating app. People who apply to or join our Creator Program (our influencer program) are a separate group, on a separate legal basis — see §13.

1.4 When your account is opened, and if sign-up is left unfinished

Your account is opened at the very start of the sign-up screens — right after you confirm the KVKK and consent step — so you have an account on our server even before you finish signing up. At that moment our server receives the account ID your phone created (and its hash) and the scrambled ID for your phone (§1.1). After that, the following goes to the server as you answer the relevant screen: your gender, gender of interest, what you're looking for (relationship / friendship), the gender you chose for friendship, your city, your country and your age range; also the name of the sign-up step you are on (for example "age range"; not your answer) and when you last took a step. Your answers to the preference questions, your photos and your messages still don't go to our server and aren't kept on it during sign-up.

You are invisible until you finish. Until sign-up is complete your account doesn't appear on anyone's list of candidates, cannot receive or make match offers, and you cannot start a round.

Reminders to finish. If you leave sign-up unfinished and your phone has a valid notification token, up to 3 notifications are sent, about 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days after your last step. The notification is fixed text (like "Pick up where you left off and finish your profile"); it carries neither the step you are on, nor a name, nor any other personal information. Each new step you take restarts the clock, but the limit of 3 does not restart. We deliver the notification through Apple's or Google's notification service and Expo (§4). If you have no notification token, no notification is sent.

An unfinished account is deleted after 90 days. If you take no step for 90 days without completing sign-up, your account is deleted from our server by the same path that runs when you delete your account yourself (§6); the check runs hourly.

Legal basis. Opening your account is the formation and performance of the contract you started (KVKK Art. 5/2-c; GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)); your explicit consent applies to your gender of interest (§9). For the reminder and the 90-day deletion the legal basis is our legitimate interest (KVKK Art. 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)): reminding someone whose sign-up is unfinished once or twice, and not keeping them forever. Your right to object is reserved (KVKK Art. 11; GDPR Art. 21).

1.5 Invite codes and campus ambassadors (launch phase)

During the launch phase an invite code may be required to get into Wybber. Whether invite-only entry is on is decided by our server; it is not a requirement built into the app and may end after the launch phase. While it is on, you can finish the sign-up screens and set up your profile without a code, but until you enter one you are invisible to others and cannot start a round. If you enter an invite code or a creator code before the KVKK and consent step (or arrive through a link), the code is only held on your phone; it is not sent to the server and is not used until you have given your consent and your account is opened.

What data — all kept under hashed account IDs, so it is pseudonymous and counts as your personal data:

  • Codes: the code itself, its owner (a campus ambassador, or the hash of your account), how many times it can be used and how many times it has been. Everyone who enters with a code receives 5 single-use codes they can share.
  • Invite record: the hash of the person who used the code (you), the code you used, the hash of the person who invited you (if you entered with an ambassador's code there is no inviter), the campus ambassador at the root of the tree, the depth (people who enter with an ambassador's code are at depth 1), the time you joined and, if you made a purchase, the time of your first paid transaction. Your account also records when you activated the code.

Why. (1) To limit access during the launch phase and to prevent a code being abused. (2) To calculate and pay a campus ambassador who has a contract with the Company the share set in that contract: from the purchases made by the accounts in their tree, for a limited period. That period is counted per phone: it starts at the first purchase seen on the phone the purchase was made on — even if it was made before the code was entered, or with another account on the same phone; for this, our server looks at the purchase and commission records that carry the phone's scrambled ID (§1.1). So deleting the account and signing up again on a phone that has already earned an ambassador a share does not restart the period. If the purchase's phone ID is unknown, the period counts from that account's first paid transaction. If an account's purchases already earned a creator a commission, the account stays with that creator even if it later enters an ambassador's code, and does not pass to the ambassador. An ordinary user earns nothing for inviting: there is no fixed per-person reward; the person who invited you and the users in between receive no share of your purchases either. The ambassador's share is paid out of the Company's revenue and doesn't change the amount you pay.

Who sees it. You can't see who used your code either; the app shows only whether a code has been used and when. A campus ambassador sees only numbers in their own panel: the total and the per-depth number of accounts in their tree, how many finished sign-up and how many are paying; the monthly ranking and the earnings estimate are also based on counts of accounts in the tree. These numbers carry no person's name, hash, account number or identity. Because an ambassador knows whom they gave codes to, the numbers are deliberately shown coarsely so that "my friend just paid" or "just finished sign-up" can't be read off a number: a number below 10 is not shown in the panel at all; numbers of 10 or more are rounded down to a multiple of 5 (for example 14 accounts show as "10"); counts based on an event cover only what happened up to the most recent UTC day boundary (00:00), so something that happens today isn't visible today. The same rule applies to the numbers in the ranking and to the numbers used in the earnings estimate; the "accounts that used the app in the last 30 days" count the estimate uses is not frozen at the day boundary, and only the below-10 hiding and the rounding protect it. To be honest: this is a display rule; the records on our server aren't rounded.

Purchase-share record. When an account in an ambassador's tree makes a purchase, the ambassador's share is recorded in the same commission record as the creators' commissions (§3, §5, §6 item 5): amount, currency, share and status; with the purchase's phone ID if it is known, or otherwise with a random value generated separately for each record (it is tied to no phone and no account), and the number of the purchase record. This record never carries your account's hash. The record is kept for 10 years under tax law and is not deleted when you delete your account; if a phone ID was written, the phone limit in §6 applies to that ID.

If you delete your account. Your invite record (the code you used, depth, root ambassador), the codes you own and, if you have one, your lifetime-plan entitlement record are deleted; the hash that names you as the "inviter" in other people's invite records is deleted too (their root ambassador and depth don't change). That severs your invite links. The one thing that remains is the purchase-share record above, which doesn't carry your account's hash.

Your data copy. It contains your invite record (code, depth, times) and the codes you own; it doesn't contain who invited you or the root ambassador, because they are third parties.

Legal basis. Running launch access and the ambassador program: performance of a contract and our legitimate interest (KVKK Art. 5/2-c and 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)); for keeping the purchase-share record, also a legal obligation (KVKK Art. 5/2-ç). Your right to object is reserved. The ambassador's own details (name, e-mail, IBAN, tax number) are kept apart from these records and described in §13.

1.6 Lifetime Infinity entitlement

Campus ambassadors who have a contract with the Company, and the first 5 people who use an ambassador's code directly, use the Infinity plan for life. This is an entitlement under a contract; it is not a purchase: no store transaction, receipt or payment record arises and it creates no commission. For this our server keeps two records tied to your account's hash: the kind of entitlement (ambassador or one of the first 5), the ambassador it is tied to and when it was granted; and the campaign record that switches your plan on. The entitlement is tied to your account, not to your phone; it survives deleting and reinstalling the app. If you delete your account, both records are deleted and the entitlement does not pass to anyone else: one of the ambassador's 5 places counts as used once (that counter is tied to no account). Your data copy contains these records; the legal basis is performance of a contract (KVKK Art. 5/2-c; GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).

1.7 Come-back reminders (optional, off by default)

If you haven't opened Wybber for a long while, we may send you at most two short reminders. We do this only if you want it: the Settings → Notifications → Notification settings → Campaigns and reminders switch is off by default, and unless you switch it on, no notification of this kind is sent to you.

  • When, and how many: at most 2 for the whole life of your account. The first goes out when you haven't opened the app for 10 days, the second at 25 days; there are at least 5 days between them. Once both have been sent, no more are ever sent, even if you come back and then stay away for a long time again; switching the setting off and on again does not reset the counter. If you reach the second threshold without having received the first (for example because you switched the setting on late), only the second is sent and the counter jumps to two.
  • Time and appearance: sent only between 09:00 and 22:00 by your phone's own clock; if it falls outside that window it is held and sent on the next hourly run if the window is open (§3.1). If Discreet notifications is on, the lock screen says only "There's something new on Wybber".
  • What the notification contains: a fixed, short text (something like "Your Wybber is still ready to work for you..."). It carries no name, number, city, date, match or anything about another person; the payload holds only the notification's type. Like the others, it is delivered through Apple's or Google's notification service and Expo (§4).
  • Which data: to choose recipients, "when you last used the app" (§3) is now also used for this purpose. Nothing else is looked at: your profile, your matches, your city and your messages play no part. Your account record stores: when you last switched the setting on, when you switched it off (if you did), the version id of the text shown next to the switch at that moment (not the text itself, only a short id), how many come-back reminders have been sent to you, and the time of the latest one. Your notification token (the push token) holds a copy of this choice of your account; the hourly job reads recipients from it. All of it is in your data copy and is deleted when you delete your account (§6).
  • The consent belongs to your account, not to your phone: only the switch in Settings changes this choice. Changing phones, a refresh of the notification token or signing in on another phone neither switches your consent on nor off; the new phone only copies your account's current state. An old "on" marker that sat only on a phone before these records existed does not count as consent either: an account with no record is sent no reminder, and consent is recorded only when you switch the setting on. When you switch the setting off, the time you switched it on and the text id are not deleted; the time of switching off is written next to them, so that we can show when consent was given, with which text, and when it was withdrawn.
  • When you log out: when you log out (or switch accounts) the app switches the setting off and reports this to our server as a withdrawal, so that someone who signs in on the same phone later does not receive reminders on your consent. The withdrawal does not block logging out: if there is no connection at that moment the logout still happens and the withdrawal may not reach our server.
  • Legal basis: your explicit consent (KVKK Art. 5/1; GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)). You switch it on yourself; it never comes pre-ticked and is not tied to any other setting or service. You can withdraw your consent at any time by switching the same setting off: your phone tells our server and from then on no notification of this kind is sent to you. Withdrawing does not affect your account, your other notifications or the lawfulness of what we did before.

2.2 Wybber Intelligence (on your phone; advanced language features not available in version 1.0)

Wybber Intelligence is Wybber's AI relationship assistant, on every plan from version 1.0. It runs on your phone and learns on your phone: it ranks who to talk to first in a round and learns which conversation ideas work for you. On-device models we add later (for example, one that learns the right time for a first conversation) are part of Wybber Intelligence too; if one of them brings a new data flow, we will update this section first. What it learns from (the results of your past first conversations, which ideas you saw, picked or skipped) is stored on your phone and is not sent to our servers; nothing is downloaded for it. Wybber Intelligence does not decide who you match with: the result of a first conversation ("match" or "no match") comes from a rule-based engine, based on both of your bars. The ranking never drops anyone from the candidate list.

The advanced language features are switched off in version 1.0. Wybber Intelligence's advanced language features suggest replies in chat (you choose and edit them; nothing is sent for you) and write a short summary of each round; the app works the same way without them. In version 1.0 the app does not offer them, nothing is downloaded for them, and none of the steps below happens. We will switch them on only after we have checked them on the phones they support, and we will update this section first if anything below changes. When they become available, they will be offered only on supported phones (iPhone 14 Pro and newer iPhones, some of them only after a short check on the phone, and a list of recent high-end Android phones), only while the app is in a language we have checked it in (English first), only on the Link and Infinity plans, and only after you choose to download them. The rest of this section describes how they work then.

The download is the only moment a third party is involved. The model is a general-purpose, publicly published file of about 1.1 GB, the same for everyone. It is downloaded only after you confirm on Settings → Matching → Device (the Wybber Intelligence card), only over Wi-Fi (never mobile data), and directly from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), which passes the request to its content-delivery network hosted on Amazon Web Services. Wybber's own servers play no part in the download. As with any download, the request shows Hugging Face and that network your IP address, the time and which file was requested, together with the standard technical information your phone sends with a request — probably the app's name and version and the version of the phone's operating system; we have not yet checked this on a device. Your profile, your messages, any text the model works on, your account ID and any other personal data are not sent. If the download is interrupted, it continues by itself from where it stopped the next time the app is open on Wi-Fi; each continuation is a new request of the same kind. If it can't continue (for example after your phone was restored from a backup), it does not start over by itself; you can start it again on the Device screen. Hugging Face handles this information under its own privacy policy (§4).

While you use it, it works without a network connection. The model runs entirely on your phone and uses no network connection. It doesn't send what it reads or writes anywhere; a suggested reply reaches your match only if you send it yourself.

Where the file is kept, and how it is removed. The file is kept only in the app's own storage on your phone and is excluded from iCloud backup (on Android, the app's storage is not backed up at all). It stays until one of these happens: you tap "Delete Wybber Intelligence" on the Device screen (available in every app language); you delete your account; you delete the app; or the app removes it by itself at start-up — once the app's language has been one it is not offered in for 30 days in a row, or if your app version or phone no longer supports the feature. Deleting your account also deletes this file, together with your confirmation to download it, so a new account on the same phone has to confirm the download again.

Legal basis. Downloading the model is a feature you ask for; showing your IP address to the server that delivers a file is technically unavoidable for any download. Legal basis: performance of our contract with you (KVKK Art. 5/2-c; GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)). For the transfer abroad, see Hugging Face in §4.

3.1 The "a chat is waiting for your reply" reminder

Your messages are end-to-end encrypted and aren't kept on our servers; we can't read their content (§2). As a result, our server cannot know by itself that a chat has gone unanswered. For this reminder to work, the phone that sent the message tells the server (using the other person's hash), and the server sends the other person a notification with fixed text.

We say it plainly: this teaches our server something it otherwise wouldn't know — "there is an unanswered chat between these two hashes". Every identifier involved is still a one-way hash, so the "only hashes reach the server" principle is not technically broken; but the state of your chat leaves your phone. That is a new kind of information, which is why it has its own section. Here is how narrow we keep it:

  • The only things stored: a one-way code made from both people's hashes (it doesn't show who reminded whom), when the last reminder for that pair was sent, and how many have been sent. Who reminded whom is written nowhere and cannot be worked out from this record.
  • Not recorded: message content, the number of unanswered messages, the times of the chat itself, or anything else about it. The time stored is when the reminder was sent, never when a message was sent.
  • The notification says nothing about the chat: a fixed title and text ("You have a conversation waiting for a reply."). It doesn't say which chat, how many messages or who sent them; the other person's phone already knows that and looks it up itself when they tap. If Discreet notifications (Settings → Notifications → Notification settings) is on, every visible notification on that phone, this one included (account and safety notices too), says only "There's something new on Wybber" on the lock screen. For this the server stores, together with your notification token, whether the setting is on (§3 table), and swaps the text when it sends.
  • Limits against harassment: at most 1 reminder per pair in total, and at most 5 requests per sender per 24 hours. That last counter is deliberately not kept in the database: it is a short-lived counter tied only to the sender's own hash and never stored next to a pair, because a permanent record would bring back exactly the "who reminded whom" that the pair code discards.
  • Time window: reminders are only sent between 09:00 and 22:00 by your phone's own clock. For this we store the name of your phone's time zone (e.g. "Europe/Istanbul") together with your notification token; a time zone is a rough location signal, and if you are travelling it can show that you are in another country. If there is no name, the time zone of the country your city is in is used (only for countries with a single time zone), and if there is none either, Türkiye time; a request that arrives outside the window is not saved for later, it is dropped. If you have more than one phone, each is judged by its own clock. Only the name is stored: no history of time offsets and no location; the name goes into your data copy together with your notification token and is deleted with it (§6).
  • Other notifications apply the same window as follows: the finish-sign-up notification, the come-back reminder (§1.7) and the "your city has opened" notification are not dropped outside the window, they are held and sent on the next hourly run if the window is open ("your city has opened" is tried for at most 3 days after the opening; the come-back reminder has no separate day limit: it is retried every hour until you open the app or switch the setting off, and it counts only once it has actually been sent). A new-match notification arrives at any hour, but quietly outside the window (on iPhone "passive": it doesn't light the screen or make a sound). New-message and "your message is waiting to be delivered" notifications (because someone is waiting for you at that moment) and account and safety notices arrive at once, whatever the time.
  • The server always gives the same answer: matched or not, blocked or not, limit reached or not. Different answers would let someone use this feature to test "does this hash have an account?", "did this pair match?" or "has the other person blocked me?".

When your phone sends it:

  • The reminder is scheduled once one of your messages has actually been sent to the other side (not if sending failed). It is cancelled the moment they reply, and dropped if you block them. It counts from your first unanswered message; later messages don't move it.
  • One attempt: 24 hours after your first unanswered message — the same as the server's limit of 1 per pair. After that attempt, nothing more is tried for that chat; so at most one reminder is ever sent for a pair.
  • An attempt counts as used as soon as it is made, and is never retried — even if your phone was offline or the request failed. Retrying depending on the answer would let the app reveal what the server deliberately hides (whether the other person has an account, whether you matched, whether they blocked you).
  • Before each attempt your phone checks again that you still have a live, mutual chat with that person; if it can't be sure, nothing is sent.
  • Your phone stores the other person only as a hash, even locally, and sends only that hash. No message content, count or chat times are stored or sent.
  • Only the server applies the 09:00–22:00 window, using the stored time-zone name (the name of the phone of the person who will receive the reminder; the sender's clock is never used). The app doesn't hold attempts back using its own clock. The honest cost: an attempt that falls outside the window is dropped and not sent later — it doesn't use up the pair's limit, but it does use your phone's only attempt, so that reminder never happens.
  • It is your choice: Settings → Notifications → Unanswered conversation reminder. This setting is on by default, and you can turn it off at any time; turning it off stops and deletes every pending reminder (they aren't saved up and sent later). We point this out because this is the one way your phone can cause a notification on someone else's phone.
  • Your choice on the receiving side: you can also turn off the reminders that reach you with Settings → Notifications → Notification settings → Chat reminders; new-message notifications still arrive.

3.2 Connecting two phones (signaling)

To connect your phone directly with your match's, the app uses a live connection to our server. Through it, the two phones exchange technical connection details (offers, answers and "ICE candidates"); our server passes them straight to the other phone and never stores them. ICE candidates contain IP addresses by nature, so at that moment your IP address passes through our server too — not only through the relay server (§3, §4).

To make the connection possible, the server keeps a few small things in its working memory (not in a database): which account IDs currently have an open connection, the purpose two accounts agreed on for a connection ("a Wybber conversation" or "a chat") for up to 10 minutes, and request counters that prevent abuse. These are keyed by your account ID as it is, not hashed.

How long: the purpose is treated as expired after 10 minutes but is actually removed only the next time it is looked up — if it is never looked up again (for example the other person never responds), the entry, which holds only the purpose label, can stay in memory. The open-connection entry lasts until the connection closes; the counters are also in memory. No separate scheduled job sweeps these; account deletion is a separate trigger.

When you delete your account: any open connection of yours is closed, and your entries in the purpose store and the counters are removed, in the same request. These steps don't touch the database; if they fail, the rest of your account is still deleted.

History: until 29 September 2026 our server also had a second, unused way to set up connections, which could keep the full connection details, including IP addresses, in memory until the server restarted. No app or website ever used it; we removed it, and an earlier version of this policy described it.

3.3 The "your message is waiting" marker

Your phone and your match's phone talk directly and end-to-end encrypted; message content isn't kept on our servers (when the phones can't connect directly, the encrypted packets pass through our relay server, which neither reads nor stores them — §3, §4). So if the other person's phone is offline (app closed, no internet), your message can't be delivered right away. For it to reach them later, our server holds not the message, only the fact that "a message is waiting". It works like this:

  1. When your message can't be delivered right away, your phone tells our server, using the other person's hash: "I have a message for this person that couldn't be delivered."
  2. The server writes a short-lived marker for those two hashes and sends the other person a notification with fixed text and no content ("You have a new message") through Apple's or Google's notification service (via Expo) (§4).
  3. When the other person opens the conversation with you (opening the app alone does not trigger this), their phone tells our server "I opened this conversation" (see below). If the server finds the marker, it gives you, the sender, a technical heads-up so you can deliver the message: over the live connection if your app is open, otherwise by silently waking your phone. Until the other person has replied to you, no visible notification saying they "opened" it or "were just here" is sent to you; after they have replied, a "Your message is waiting to be delivered" notification may be shown. If they haven't replied, your message goes out when you open the app or when your phone is silently woken. Your phones connect directly and the message is delivered directly, phone to phone. The marker is then deleted. If the server can't use the marker at that moment (for example it has no way to reach you), the marker is not deleted; it is tried again the next time the other person opens that conversation, and expires by itself after 7 days at the latest.

We say it plainly: this tells our server "the person with this hash has a message that couldn't be delivered to the person with that hash". The identifiers are still one-way hashes, but a fact about your chat leaves your phone with its direction and time. It is a deliberate cost: we can't get a message to someone offline without knowing this. Unlike the reminder in §3.1, the direction is kept (from whom to whom), because the server has to know whom to wake.

What our server learns: the two hashes; the direction (who wrote, to whom); the time the marker was made. When the marker is used is not recorded separately; the marker is deleted once it is used.

What it doesn't learn: the content or text; how many messages are waiting (one or ten — it's the same marker); whether it was read; anything else about the chat.

What it additionally learns when you open a conversation: when you open a conversation, your phone sends our server the hash of the person in that conversation and a single yes/no fact: whether you have already replied to them. Your phone sends this for every conversation you open, whether or not a message is waiting (at most once every 5 minutes for the same person). The server uses it only to look up whether that person left a marker and, if so, to act on it. So for a brief moment the server knows "this account opened its conversation with the account with this hash"; if a marker exists, it is acted on and consumed at once. It does not store the opening itself — nothing is written to a database table or a marker, so which conversation you opened and when is not kept (only a technical error type with no identity can reach the application log). Beyond that there are three things: a request counter tied only to your own hash and kept for 24 hours (at most 300 requests a day; it doesn't say whose conversation you opened); a total counter, tied to no person or account, that only counts how many requests arrived (§3.4); and the ordinary server log that applies to every request (your IP address, the time and the address requested — the other person's hash is inside the request, not in the address; §3). The server always gives the same answer to this request, so the feature can't be used to test "does this hash have an account?" or "did we match?".

How long: at most 7 days. It is deleted as soon as it is used; if it isn't used within 7 days, it expires by itself. It is not written to a database table.

Limits against abuse: the times of notifications sent to a pair and to one person, and your number of requests per day (separately for "undelivered" and "I opened a conversation" requests), are kept for at most 24 hours, with no content.

Notifications carry fixed text, never message content. This feature lets your phone cause a notification on someone else's phone — we say so for the same reason as in §3.1.

From the other side: if a match left you a message that couldn't be delivered, their phone is given a technical heads-up the moment you open your conversation with them (so it can deliver the message); opening the app alone does not trigger this. Until you reply to them, no visible notification is sent to them; after you reply, a "Your message is waiting to be delivered" notification may be shown. The way to stop this is to block that person (if you have blocked them, the marker is deleted without being acted on). You can switch off the visible notifications (for example "You have a new message" and "Your message is waiting to be delivered") on your own phone under Notifications → Preferences → Messages; that preference is stored on the server with your push token, and a type you switched off is not sent to you. That setting does not stop the silent wake needed to deliver the message — only blocking stops that — and quiet hours don't apply to these notifications (unlike the §3.1 reminder, which is only sent 09:00–22:00).

Account deletion: markers where you are the sender or the recipient, and abuse-limit records tied to you, are deleted immediately; if that part of our system is briefly unreachable, markers expire within at most 7 days and the records within 24 hours. Pair records (a one-way code made from two people) expire within 24 hours.

Why: so that a message you write to someone who is offline actually reaches them when they come back.

3.4 Service statistics — totals not linked to anyone

To run Wybber and notice when something breaks, our server counts events it sees itself — how many phones connected per hour, how many Wybber rounds started, how many matches happened, how many notifications went out, how many new accounts were created per day. It counts how many, not who did what.

These numbers are not linked to anyone. A counter row holds only a time slot (minute, hour or day), a counter name, at most two broad labels (for example iPhone/Android, plan type, "match"/"no match") and a number. It contains no account ID, hash, account number, device ID, who-with-whom, IP address or content. Our code enforces this: a label that looks like an ID or a hash (for example 12 or more hexadecimal characters in a row) is rejected — the rejection is counted, and the value itself is written nowhere. Nothing is sent from your phone for this purpose — the one exception is the type name sent when you tap a notification (see below); the rest are events our server already sees. These totals are not part of the usage data in §8 and are not combined with it.

Notification counters. To see whether notifications work, our server counts, per notification type (for example "new match"), how many notifications were sent, how many were not sent (because there was no notification token, the type was switched off, or it was outside the time window) and how many times the app was opened by tapping a notification. A counter is only a type name, an outcome and a number; it has no account ID, hash, notification token or who tapped. When you tap a notification, your phone sends the server only this type name; the server doesn't record which account it came from. To stop this request being abused, an hourly request counter tied to your internal account number is kept (at most 60 per hour); it is only a number and deletes itself after 1 hour.

Who sees them: only Wybber operators with access to our server, on an admin screen. On that screen numbers below 5 (below 10 when broken down by city) are hidden. To be clear, this is a display rule: the stored counters are not deleted or blurred, and it doesn't limit what an operator with direct server access can see — but the counters aren't linked to anyone in the first place.

City counts (opening of rounds). To decide when Wybber rounds open in a city and when the limits of the paid plans start to apply, our server calculates the number of active accounts per city from the city and last-use time on account records (Terms of Service §7). This is only a total: no new data is collected and the result doesn't point to any single person.

Three markers on your account: so that we can see, in total, how many people get from signing up to a first match and a first chat, your account record holds three yes/no markers: whether your account has started its first Wybber round, had its first match and opened its first chat. Each is only yes or no: no date or time is kept, and nothing about who the other person was, how often, or any other detail. Because these are linked to you, unlike the totals above, we treat them as your data: they are in your data copy and are deleted with your account (§6).

Active-day record: on the days you open the app in the foreground (when you sign up, sign in or your session is refreshed), our server keeps only your internal account number and that day's date (UTC, no time of day); no IP address, device or location is added to the record. The app running in the background (for example, being woken by a silent notification) does not count as an active day. Purpose: to work out, in aggregate, how many people use Wybber per day, week and month, and whether new accounts come back on day 1, 7 and 30. Reports show only totals (with breakdowns by iPhone/Android and plan type); groups smaller than 5 are hidden. Like the three markers, this record is linked to your account and we treat it as your data. Retention: 45 days, then deleted automatically every day. If you delete your account, it is deleted immediately; it is included in your data copy (§6). The totals calculated from it are, like the counters above, not linked to anyone and are not changed by account deletion. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running and improving the service (KVKK Art. 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)); you have the right to object (KVKK Art. 11; GDPR Art. 21).

Gender and gender-of-interest totals: with your explicit consent (Explicit Consent Notice §1), the split by gender and gender of interest is shown on the admin screen only as live totals, rounded to 10; these numbers are not stored, no history is kept, and no breakdown narrow enough to point to one person is shown. This applies only to people who gave consent after the version of Explicit Consent Notice §1 that includes it took effect; an earlier consent does not cover it, and those accounts aren't counted. If you withdraw that consent (Settings → Privacy and safety → Gender and interest-gender consent), this information (your gender, your gender of interest and, if you gave it, who you want to meet as friends) is deleted from our server (§9) and you are no longer counted. Apart from this count, your gender and gender of interest are not used in service statistics.

How long: minute-level snapshots 30 days, hourly counters 90 days, daily totals 2 years; older rows are deleted automatically (§5). The three markers are kept for as long as your account exists, the active-day record for 45 days.

Account deletion: because the counter rows can't be linked to any account, deleting your account doesn't change them — your account's contribution to the totals (for example "one account was created") can't be undone. The three markers and your active-day record are deleted with your account.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running the service safely and noticing faults (KVKK Art. 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). Because the totals aren't linked to anyone and only a notification-type name is taken from your phone, we consider that the balance doesn't tip against you. The gender and gender-of-interest totals are different: they rest on your explicit consent, as described above. You have the right to object to the three markers and the active-day record on your account (KVKK Art. 11; GDPR Art. 21) — write to support@wybber.com.

6. Account deletion and data portability

You can delete your account at any time: Settings → Delete account. One request does all of the following on our server, together:

  1. Deleted for good: your account record (including your province/district, search settings, province-change times and Travel Mode entry — §1.2, §1.3), your blocks, your notification token, its language and your notification preferences, your location-update times, your location preference, your usage records, your conversation records, your one-sided match signals and confirmed matches, your retry pauses, the reminder records of pairs you belong to (§3.1), usage data linked to your account (§8), the feedback you sent, the three account markers and your active-day record (§3.4), your app integrity records (§3), your invite-tree records and your lifetime-entitlement record (§1.5, §1.6; the hash that names you as the "inviter" in other people's invite records is deleted too, which severs your invite links) and your sign-up step (§1.4, part of your account record), together with the proof of your come-back reminder consent (your times of switching on and off, the version id of the text), the counter and the time of the latest one (§1.7; all of it is deleted with your account record, the copy of the choice with your notification token). Short-lived records (§3.3): message-waiting markers where you are the sender or the recipient, and abuse-limit records tied to you, are deleted immediately; if that part of our system is briefly unreachable, markers expire within 7 days and the records within 24 hours; pair records expire within 24 hours.
  2. Kept, but the link to you is severed for good — your account ID in these records is replaced with a one-way pseudonym, so they can no longer be tied to your account:

- Payment and purchase records — kept as the financial audit trail; the link to your account is severed and the phone ID on them is cleared (but see the limit about commission records below). - Safety reports (filed by you or about you) — kept so that moderation history stays intact; in them, your ID is replaced with a pseudonym that only our server key can link back to it (this is a pseudonym and stays personal data; the other person's ID doesn't change unless they delete their own account), and the reports are deleted on the schedule in §5. - Free-trial record — the record itself is not deleted: deleting it would free your phone's trial and let a new account take another one. Your account and any creator link on it are severed; only your phone's scrambled ID, where known the note of where that ID came from, and the trial's dates and length remain.

  1. Signed out: the session used for the request is cancelled immediately, and any open connection of yours is closed.
  2. Device record — not part of this action (§1.1): it never held your account identity, so there is no link to sever. It stays for its own period — 90 days after the last account registered on that phone — so that deleting and reinstalling can't reset a free trial.
  3. Four kinds of record that account deletion does not touch at all. It neither deletes them nor cuts a link, because none of them has a separate account field to cut (the suspension record is explained in its own bullet). We list them here because this is where you would ask:

- Referral-code record — your phone's scrambled ID, which creator code was used, the platform and the install date. It stays for the same reason as the device record in item 4: deleting it would let the same phone redeem a referral code again. No set deletion period. - Commission record — your phone's scrambled ID, the purchase amount and the commission a creator earned from it. It is kept because the law requires it: 10 years under VUK Art. 253 and TTK Art. 82 (§13.4 is the single source for the period). During that time it cannot be fully deleted, and it is outside the scope of any "delete my account/data" request — the same commitment §13.4 makes to creators. Because it also holds a third party's commercial data (the creator's commission), it is left out of your data copy too, and that second reason doesn't end when the 10 years do. We say it honestly: it is also your own pseudonymous personal data — we don't claim otherwise; keeping it and not showing it rest on the legal duty and on the third party's rights. - Store notification receipts — records that an Apple or Google subscription notification was processed. They hold only the store's own notification and transaction numbers and no account field at all; they are not deleted, because they stop the same notification from being processed twice and serve as a billing audit trail. Their only possible link to you is through the purchase record whose link is severed in item 2. No set deletion period. - Suspension record — the audit record of an account being suspended or reinstated by us: a hash of the account ID (this is the record's own reference, not a separate link), the action, a reason code from a fixed list (for example "found to be under 18" — §11 — or "abuse confirmed"), the internal username of the Wybber operator who acted, and when. Why it is retained: it is the only proof that the promise in §11 (accounts of people under 18 are closed) and our other sanctions were really applied; if a suspended account could erase it by deleting itself, the sanction would look as if it never happened — the same principle as for the reports in item 2 and the other records in this item. The operator's username can't contain an e-mail address, spaces or free text, but it can be based on a real name (for example "mehmet.yilmaz"); so this record is your personal data and may also be pseudonymous personal data of that operator. No set deletion period (§5). Legal basis: legitimate interest (KVKK Art. 5/2-f; GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)); keeping it after a deletion request falls under KVKK Art. 7 and GDPR Art. 17(3)(e) (establishing, exercising or defending legal claims) — it protects against a dispute or an appeal over the suspension.

The same honest limit for the referral-code and commission records. These records hold your phone's scrambled ID, not your account. If you open a new account on the same phone, it may record the same phone ID (we deliberately allow a second, legitimate account on one phone). So these records lose their link to your account, but not to your phone — which is exactly what preventing abuse and keeping accounts requires. We don't claim "these can no longer be linked to anyone".

Campus-ambassador share records. The commission record kept for a purchase in a campus ambassador's tree (§1.5) isn't deleted either, but it never carries your account's hash: it holds the purchase's phone ID if that is known, or otherwise a random value generated separately for each record. If a phone ID was written, the phone limit above applies; if a random value was written, the record can be linked neither to your phone nor to your account. We don't delete the record because of our duty to keep it for 10 years (§13.4).

The suspension record works differently — we say so plainly. It holds the hash of your account ID itself, and that hash is not changed when you delete your account (unlike item 2, it isn't turned into a pseudonym). While your account exists, the same hash also appears in several other records (your feedback, match signals, matches, usage data, usage records and age-check risk signals); when you delete your account all of those are deleted or turned into a separate pseudonym, so afterwards this is the only record that still carries it. And because your account ID is derived from your 12 recovery words, restoring your account with the same words recreates the same ID, so the old record would point to the restored account again — that is how restoring is designed to work, not a rare edge case. What it does not do: it doesn't suspend a restored account automatically. Only an administrator's action writes this record and only administrators can read it; nothing sets an account's status from it.

And this limit narrows item 2. A commission record also points to the purchase it came from (it holds that purchase's internal number and the store's transaction number). Item 2 is true: the purchase record's own account link is replaced by a pseudonym and the phone ID on it is cleared. But for a purchase that earned a commission, the purchase record can still be reached in two steps — phone ID → commission record → purchase record — which can show details such as which product was bought. The purchase record reached this way does not name you or your account (its account identity really has been replaced for good), but it can be linked to the phone, through an account opened later on the same phone. This applies only to purchases made through a creator's code and to campus-ambassador share records that carry a phone ID; from a campus record that carries a random value the phone can't be reached, and other purchases have no commission record and so no such path.

Why we explain this instead of "fixing" it: the only fix would be to delete the commission record or cut its links, and that would break the legal duty to keep it (§13.4; VUK Art. 253 / TTK Art. 82). So the choice is not between closing a gap and obeying the law; it is between telling you about a limit created by a record we must keep, and not telling you. We are telling you.

What happens on your phone. All of the above is about our server. The app also removes data from your phone — but only after our server has confirmed the deletion (or confirmed that the account was already gone). If the request can't reach the server or is refused, nothing on your phone is touched and your account stays as it was.

*Removed from your phone:*

  • The app's local storage — your profile, preference answers, consent records, block list, chat-session and match data, and the profile cards you received. All of the app's local storage is cleared; the only thing written back is a single marker saying the account no longer exists on the server (no personal data).
  • The app's local databases — your sign-up answers (including gender, gender of interest, city/district, age range and name), safety logs, consent records and audit logs. The rows are cleared first, then the database files are deleted together with their companion files.
  • Photos, voice and face files — your profile photo (stored encrypted on the phone), voice messages, the video and photo taken during face verification, temporary copies of photos you picked or sent, and any data-copy file the app prepared for you.
  • Keys in the system keychain — your account keys, your recovery-word data, both sign-in token entries (your sign-in token and, on iPhone, the short-lived background sign-in token used while the phone is locked), the app's local encryption key and (on iPhone) the ID of the app-integrity (App Attest) key. iOS manages the App Attest key itself and no app can delete it; the app forgets its ID, so an account opened later on this phone creates a new key.

*Deliberately kept on your phone:* the speed-test results of the device-performance screen (timings and memory figures for this phone, plus the model's output on a fixed built-in sample text) and the database that lists Wybber Intelligence files. None of them contains your profile, messages or photos; they are kept only because they hold nothing derived from you, and if that ever stops being true, this exception ends. Wybber Intelligence's downloaded file (and anything stored with it) is deleted with your account (§2.2).

*What this does not mean — the limits:*

  1. This is ordinary deletion, not overwriting. The app deletes files and keychain items; it doesn't first overwrite their contents. Storage blocks can physically remain until the phone reuses them, so we can't rule out specialist forensic recovery from a phone that is in someone else's hands.
  2. iPhone: one key isn't removed by deleting the app. The app's local encryption key lives in the system keychain. The app deletes it and checks that it's gone; if it isn't, the app tells you. On iPhone, deleting an app does not remove keychain items, and no app code runs while an app is being deleted — so deleting and reinstalling Wybber doesn't remove that key. If the app says it couldn't remove it, the only reliable fix on iPhone is to erase the iPhone (Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Erase All Content and Settings). On Android, deleting the app clears its private storage and its keystore entry together. The key only opens the encrypted data that was stored on this phone, so once that data is gone it has nothing left to open.
  3. Copies we can't reach aren't deleted: phone backups made before the deletion (iCloud, your computer, Google backup); the originals in your photo library; anything you saved or shared outside the app (for example a data copy you saved to Files or sent to another app); and content already delivered, end-to-end encrypted, to someone else's phone — we can't recall it.
  4. If a step fails, the app tells you. You're told that your account was deleted but some data couldn't be removed from the phone, and you can try again. We don't report a clean deletion when we know part of it failed.
  5. This section is based on the app's source code and on the source code of the libraries it uses to create these files. A file-level check of a real phone after deletion has not been carried out yet; we don't claim that it has.

The same removal from the phone also runs when you restore a *different* account onto a phone with recovery words: the previous account's data is removed from that phone only — its account on our server is not deleted — and if part of that removal fails, the person now using the phone is told that data from the previous account may still be on it.

8. Usage data (telemetry) — the honest picture

Today the app sends no general usage data. The app contains a usage-data component (it would send things like crash and error counts and feature use, with a device ID and a session ID), but it is switched off and nothing in the app switches it on. If we ever switch it on, we will update this section first. We say plainly what it would lack today: our design aims to protect usage data with differential privacy (adding statistical noise so that no individual can be picked out, with ε ≤ 1.0), but this component adds no noise, would carry precise timestamps and a device ID, and sends signed batches. Usage events about Wybber conversations stay switched off completely until a properly protected pipeline exists. Any usage rows linked to your account by a hash are deleted when you delete your account (§5, §6); older rows without that link (from before 19 September 2026, or from an app version that didn't add it) can't be linked to any account and so can't be deleted.

What is sent: a few technical counts without your account ID.

  • Connection-reliability counts: when a chat connection ends abnormally (for example it couldn't be set up, or reconnecting failed), the app sends a scrambled, random session number with counts — how long the session lasted, how many messages were sent in it, and how many reconnects, connection checks and idle time-outs happened. No content, no account ID, no device ID. Our server keeps these only in its working memory (at most the latest 1,000), they are lost when it restarts, and like any request they appear in our server logs with your IP address (§3).

10. Security

  • Sign-in: your account is a cryptographic key pair created on your phone (Ed25519); there is no password.
  • Messages: end-to-end encrypted (X25519 key exchange and XSalsa20-Poly1305).
  • Identifiers sent to our server are hashed (SHA-256), apart from the deliberate exceptions above.
  • Connections to our server use HTTPS/TLS, and we run our own relay server; both were checked by an automated test in production.

11. Children

Wybber is only for adults. The youngest age range at sign-up is 18-22. Accounts found to belong to someone under 18 are closed.

The administrative record of such a closure (which account, when, why, and the internal username of the operator who closed it) is kept even if the account is later deleted — see §5 and §6 item 5 for what it contains, why it is kept and its legal basis. A closed account no longer appears as a candidate, can't make new connections and can't reach its chat history. However, a closure may take effect only when the current sign-in session runs out: some parts of our service check the account's status on every request (there the closure applies immediately), others rely on the session alone until it expires, and how long that takes depends on the setup, so we don't state a single number. This is different from deleting an account, where the session is cancelled immediately (§6 item 3); the same guarantee doesn't yet exist for closures, and this is an open technical issue.

13. Creator Program (influencer) data — a separate group

This section is only about people who apply to or join the Wybber Creator Program (our influencer program). It doesn't affect dating users who never use that program. §1–§12 cover account holders who use Wybber as a dating app; this section covers a separate group and a separate legal relationship (a business partnership, not consent). If you applied through the "Wybber Ambassadors" form on our website, the rules specific to that form are in §13.7; that application is not linked to a dating account.

The two groups' data is not merged directly — but there are links, explained here. None of the program's own records (applications, creator accounts, payout details, sign-in links) contains a link to a dating account. For a phone that came through a creator's invite code, however, there are two links. (a) Attribution: the invite-code and commission records in §3 link your phone's scrambled ID to the creator who owns the code; the creator's share is calculated from these records. This is the same on iPhone and on Android. (b) Trial length, Android only: if our server knows from its own records that the phone is an Android phone and the code was entered on Android, the code extends the free trial from 7 to 14 days; the free-trial record in §3 then also links the account to the creator who owns the code. On iPhone an invite code is for attribution only: it gives no plan, trial or free time; the trial is 7 days and the free-trial record has no link to a creator. These links are deliberate and necessary: without attribution the creator's share can't be calculated, and on Android the server can't decide between a 7-day and a 14-day trial without knowing whether the phone came through a creator's code. The links are limited: (1) they exist only inside our server and never appear in anything the app shows to you or to anyone else; (2) the creator can never see you (§13.1, "Not collected"); (3) when you delete your account, the link in the free-trial record is severed, while the invite-code and commission records stay tied to your phone, not to your account (§6). Deleting a dating account never touches the program's own records.
